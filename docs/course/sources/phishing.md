# Phishing module source notes

Checked 2026-10-20. These are editorial references for five newly authored lesson pairs. Scenarios, people, addresses and dialogue in the lessons are synthetic; none is a reported incident. The lead owns the separate `lookalike-domains` pilot and its citations.

| Lesson | Official primary source | Claim supported and scope |
|---|---|---|
| email-anatomy, link-previews, quishing | [UK NCSC, spotting scam messages](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/spot-scams) | Published 2021, reviewed 2022; urgency/authority are cues, poor spelling is not a dependable test, verify through contact details found separately, and QR codes in emails can be a phishing route. UK reporting links are local. The page is live guidance but its formal review date is older. |
| email-anatomy, reporting-suspicious | [US FTC, recognizing phishing](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-avoid-phishing-scams) | Unexpected account/payment/attachment pretexts, independent contact, reporting and post-interaction action. Reporting and identity-theft routes are US-specific. No prevalence number from this page is used. |
| email-anatomy, link-previews, fake-login-pages, reporting-suspicious | [Gmail Help, avoid and report phishing](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/8253?hl=en) | Gmail's desktop hover check, direct navigation instead of entering a password after a message link, the web report control, and Google's notice that manually reported email/attachments may be copied for analysis. These are Gmail behaviors, not universal mail-client controls. |
| link-previews | [Apple Support, preview website links in Safari on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/iphone/iph1fbef4daa/26/ios/26) | Long-press preview in Safari for iOS 26. Other browsers/apps/devices may show a different interface or truncated address. |
| fake-login-pages | [UK NCSC, password manager buyers guide](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/passwords/password-manager-buyers-guide) | Autofill should match the saved site and can help resist phishing. This guidance is for system owners; it is not a claim that every manager always detects a fake page or that autofill refusal diagnoses one. |
| fake-login-pages | [US FTC, public Wi-Fi guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/are-public-wi-fi-networks-safe-what-you-need-know) | HTTPS can encrypt traffic to a fake website without making information safe from its operator. US consumer context; no Wi-Fi-specific claim is generalized. |
| quishing | [US FTC, QR stickers on parking meters](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/09/see-qr-code-parked-somewhere-dont-scan-ityet) | September 2026 report of overlaid parking QR codes, link preview and actions after entering credentials/card details. US reporting and payment context. A sticker is a warning, not proof of a specific crime. |
| reporting-suspicious | [UK NCSC, report a scam email](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-email) | UK Suspicious Email Reporting Service, `report@phishing.gov.uk`, and guidance to report doubtful mail without following links. Not a worldwide reporting destination or a guarantee of takedown. |
| reporting-suspicious | [Microsoft Support, phishing in Outlook](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/outlook/mail/phishing-and-suspicious-behavior-in-outlook) | Outlook.com `Report → Report phishing`; reporting a sender does not itself block future mail. Service-specific menu can change. |

An older FTC QR article timed out when its full page was opened, so it is not cited in the authored lessons. A Microsoft `protect-yourself-from-phishing` URL currently redirects to a different Microsoft Learn article whose reporting details are inconsistent with current Outlook support; it is also excluded. No third-party short-URL expanding service is recommended, because it can disclose the submitted URL and is unnecessary when a known route exists.
