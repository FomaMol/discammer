# Passwords, MFA and account recovery — source ledger

Checked against the actual linked primary-source pages on **2026-10-20**. This ledger supports six paired lessons. EN and RU lesson references use the same URLs and localised scope notes. All cases and practice accounts are synthetic; `support-example.test` uses the reserved `.test` namespace. No passwords, codes, user messages or financial details are collected by the lessons.

| Source | Specific claim used | Scope and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [NIST SP 800-63B-4, Authenticators](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) | Manual OTP entry is not phishing-resistant; correctly configured WebAuthn binds an authenticator response to a service identifier; password hashes and password verification have different properties from public-key credentials. | Current US federal technical standard, published 2025; adapted as general mechanism, not a claim that every site conforms. |
| [NIST SP 800-63B-4, Account Recovery](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#account-recovery) | Recovery is a separate process after authenticator loss and may use codes, contacts or renewed proofing; a recovery event merits notification. | US federal design requirements; consumer-provider procedures and wait times vary. |
| [NIST SP 800-63B-4, Syncable Authenticators](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#syncable-authenticators) | Syncable keys can be phishing-resistant when correctly configured, but may be exportable/synced and require protection of the sync fabric. | US federal design requirements; not a blanket claim about every passkey vendor's implementation. |
| [FTC, Use Two-Factor Authentication](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/use-two-factor-authentication-protect-your-accounts) | SMS is better than no second step where that is the only option; app codes avoid SIM-swap delivery but remain manual codes; security keys are another option. | US consumer guidance; available methods vary by service. |
| [FTC, SIM Swap Scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2019/10/sim-swap-scams-how-protect-yourself) | SIM replacement can deliver a victim's texts to an attacker; carrier account PIN and stronger MFA are possible safeguards. | US consumer alert from 2019; checked live 2026-10-20, carrier features are jurisdiction-specific. |
| [FTC, How To Recover Your Hacked Email or Social Media Account](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recover-your-hacked-email-or-social-media-account) | After reclaiming an account, change its password, sign out devices, inspect recovery contacts and forwarding, and warn contacts when appropriate. | US consumer guidance, not a guaranteed sequence or vendor-specific procedure. |
| [UK NCSC, Password Manager Buyers Guide](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/passwords/password-manager-buyers-guide) | Evaluate vault encryption, access and recovery, MFA and the risk of unencrypted exports. | Guidance to organisational system owners; consumer advice is framed as questions to ask, not a product endorsement. |
| [UK NCSC, Securing Your Users' Accounts](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/using-online-services-safely/securing-your-users-accounts) | Unique passwords contain cross-account damage; browser or app managers can support them; primary passphrases should avoid guessable personal data. | UK small-business guidance, published/reviewed 2024; not a rule that every user must use a specific manager. |
| [Have I Been Pwned FAQ](https://haveibeenpwned.com/Faqs) | Breach lookup indexes an email address or username for the listed incident. It does not pair that identifier with a password; Pwned Passwords is separate. A listed result needs the provider's data-category details, while no result is not proof of safety. | Documentation of HIBP's own service and dataset; not a complete threat feed or proof of account compromise. The credential-audit shop example explicitly includes exposed email addresses and says passwords were not reported exposed. |
| [Google Account Help, passkey sign-in](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/13548313?hl=en) | Create passkeys on personally controlled devices; Google's settings list credentials and allow removal, with specific device-dependent steps. | Google-account procedure only; not presented as universal for all passkey services. |

## Corrections to previous module text

- Removed unsupported universal breach counts, guessed prices, cracking time promises and “your password is already out there” claim. Breach content varies; hashes are not automatically plaintext.
- Changed “autofill refusal proves phishing” to a prompt for independent verification. Legitimate form or subdomain changes can also prevent autofill.
- Changed “all passkeys are synced, non-exportable, in secure enclaves, and lost with one platform account” to provider-dependent storage and recovery. A passkey can be synced or device-bound.
- Changed the strict MFA ladder and “SMS must never be the only option” to strongest supported method plus explicit fallback assessment. If SMS is the only offered second factor, it is generally preferable to password-only login.
- Changed “hardware key can always be the only recovery path” and universal carrier port-lock claims to service- and carrier-dependent controls.
- Replaced annual mass password rotation with an impact-driven audit of reuse, known compromise, recovery, sessions, connected apps and tokens. Review frequency remains a personal reminder, not a proven one-hour outcome.
- Reconciled the synthetic shop breach: its notice now says names and email addresses were exposed, but no passwords were reported exposed. This matches a HIBP lookup for that email without claiming a password leak.
