# Social engineering module — editorial source notes

Checked live on **2026-10-20**. All lesson cases and practice situations are synthetic. These sources support patterns and safe decisions; they do not certify any fictional person, message, organization, or payment. A warning cue prompts verification, not an automatic fraud verdict. Country-specific reporting and legal processes are not transferred across jurisdictions.

## Corrections to prior copy

- Removed unsupported global prevalence, “2% hit rate,” six- and seven-figure loss generalizations, and the claim that labeling a tactic makes it stop working.
- Replaced claims that *all* operations follow one five-stage sequence, that a small withdrawal happens exactly once, or that every new online contact is fraudulent. The SEC-led 2024 alert supports possible small withdrawals, not a fixed script; the broader relationship-scam overview does not state that narrower claim.
- Removed fixed voice-cloning sample lengths and costs. FTC supports the possibility of a short clip, not a guaranteed real-time clone from a specified number of seconds.
- Removed “never/always” assertions about genuine banks, police, support tools, callers' reactions, and universal time limits. The stable recommendation is independent confirmation of the specific request through an established channel.
- Preserved the useful six-principle framework, pretext analysis, phone/text/voice threats, romance-investment pattern, conversation cues and practical verification routine, with explicit limitations and legitimate confusing cases.
