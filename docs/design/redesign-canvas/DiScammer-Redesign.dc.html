The “safe account” call

A caller posing as your bank’s security team convinces you to move your own money to a “protected” account they control. No malware, no stolen card, you press every button yourself. That is exactly why it works.

ARTICLE field note · analysis

Anatomy of a deepfake-voice call: where the money actually leaves

Cloned voices are the part that makes headlines. But the voice is just the door. The step worth memorising is the one where the money leaves your control, and it has not changed in years.

DS
DiScammer desk
2026-06-12 · 8 min read
A FIG.1 · CALL FLOW

You get a call. The voice is your daughter, or your manager, or someone from your bank. It is convincing because it is built from a few seconds of real audio, scraped from a voice message or a video. The technology is genuinely good now. Arguing about whether it sounds real is a trap, because it will.

So drop that question. The useful question is never “is this really them?” It is “what am I being asked to do?” Every one of these calls ends at the same place: a request to move money or share a code. That request is the part you can catch.

The screenshot people send us

Here is a real example a reader forwarded, with the personal details removed. Read what it is actually asking for, underneath the panic it is trying to create.

SMS · +7 XXX XXX XX 14 · 09:42

Mom it’s me, I’m in trouble and lost my phone. Please don’t call, just transfer 45,000 to this card 2202 •••• •••• 8841 right now, I’ll explain everything later.

▸ don’t call = isolation ▸ right now = urgency ▸ transfer = the ask

The voice can be faked. The action cannot be undone. So slow down the action, not the voice.

What to do instead

Pick a private question only the real person could answer, agreed in advance with your family. Then hang up and reach them on a number you already have. If it is real, a thirty-second delay costs nothing. If it is not, that delay is the whole game.
