A message offers remote work at good pay, and the recruiter is in a hurry to sign you up.

Remote work is real, and scammers know it. They borrow its job listings, recruiter messages and official-looking paperwork to get at your money or personal information. A real offer gets easier to check as it moves toward an interview and a hire, not harder.

## Five signs the offer does not add up

1. **An offer that skips the interview.** If an unsolicited message asks for paperwork before anyone has talked about the duties, pause.
2. **A sender you cannot match to the employer.** Check the recruiter and the company through contact details you find yourself.
3. **Paying to work.** Fees for training, equipment, certification, directories or a “guaranteed” job are warning signs.
4. **Sensitive data asked for too early.** Bank details, identity documents and tax information should not go to someone you have not verified.
5. **Receive money or goods, then pass them on.** Depositing a check, buying equipment, reshipping goods or wiring money can draw you into fraud.

## An invented example: the payroll text

A text offers remote data-entry work for high pay. Before any real interview, the sender asks for your bank details “for payroll” and says you must buy equipment with a check they will send you. Do not hand over the details, do not deposit the check and do not send money back. Find the employer's official careers page and use a route you have verified to ask whether the role exists.

## Safer ways to apply

1. Apply through the employer's official careers site, or confirm a recruiter by a contact method you found yourself.
2. Search for the company and the recruiter's name together with “scam,” “complaint” or “review.”
3. Ask for a clear job description, the interview process, how the pay works and the terms in writing.
4. Do not pay to get a job, and do not forward money as part of an assignment.
5. Keep copies of the listing and the messages in case you need to report the offer.

If you have already shared information or sent money, contact the relevant bank, payment service or identity-protection authority promptly. Employment and consumer-protection rules differ from country to country.
