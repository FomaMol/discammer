Remote work is real, but scammers also use job listings, recruiter messages, and official-looking paperwork to seek money or personal information. A job offer should become more verifiable as it moves toward an interview and hiring, not less.

## Warning signs

1. **A fast offer with little interview process.** An unsolicited offer that asks for paperwork before discussing duties is a reason to pause.
2. **A sender who cannot be matched to the employer.** Check the recruiter and company through contact details you find independently.
3. **A request to pay in order to work.** Fees for training, equipment, certification, directories, or a “guaranteed” job are warning signs.
4. **Sensitive data requested too early.** Bank details, identity documents, and tax information should not be sent to an unverified contact.
5. **A request to receive money or goods and forward them.** Depositing a check, buying equipment, reshipping goods, or wiring money can involve you in fraud.

## Illustrative scenario — not a case report

You receive a text offering remote data-entry work for high pay. Before a real interview, the sender asks for your bank details for “payroll” and says you must buy equipment using a check they will send. Do not provide data, deposit the check, or send money back. Find the employer’s official careers page and contact it through a verified route to ask whether the role exists.

## Safer application steps

1. Apply through the employer’s official careers site or confirm a recruiter using a contact method you find yourself.
2. Search the company and recruiter name with “scam,” “complaint,” or “review.”
3. Ask for a clear job description, the interview process, pay arrangement, and written terms.
4. Do not pay to get a job and do not forward money as part of an assignment.
5. Keep copies of the listing and messages if you need to report the offer.

If you shared information or money, contact the relevant bank, payment service, or identity-protection authority promptly. Employment and consumer-protection rules vary by country.
