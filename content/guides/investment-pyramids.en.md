The group chat is full of screenshots: a banking app, a payout, a row of thumbs-up underneath. Everyone seems to be getting paid.

A pyramid scheme runs mainly on money from new participants, not on demonstrated sales to ordinary customers. A Ponzi scheme pays its early investors out of what later ones put in. Either can look convincing at the start: the early payments are real enough to screenshot, and polished marketing sells them as other people's approval.

## Five signs worth a second look

1. **Recruiting is the main way to earn.** Be wary when rewards depend more on the people you bring in than on sales to customers outside the program.
2. **High returns, little or no risk.** Every investment carries risk, so treat a “guaranteed” return as a claim to verify independently.
3. **Revenue you cannot see.** Ask what the product or service is, who buys it, and where the income comes from.
4. **Fees and commissions nobody can explain.** If you cannot describe in plain words how the payments work, do not join.
5. **Pressure to buy stock, training or a place in the scheme.** A compulsory buy-in is part of the risk. It is not evidence of an opportunity.

## An invented example: the payout screenshots

An online group promises quick returns and posts screenshots of payouts. To take part you pay an entry fee and bring in several people, and nobody can show independently audited sales to customers outside the group. Treat the screenshots as marketing, not verification. Hold off on any transfer until you have checked the registration, the terms, how compensation is calculated and the business model, all through sources the group did not hand you.

## Before any money moves

1. Take your time. If the offer expires soon, let it.
2. Look up the company, and anyone advising you on it, with the financial regulator in your country.
3. Ask for written details of the risks, fees, revenue and how compensation is calculated.
4. Look for evidence of real sales to people outside the recruitment network.
5. Get an independent opinion before you invest money you cannot afford to lose.

This guide describes common warning signs. It cannot tell you whether a particular company is lawful or safe, and investment rules and reporting routes differ from country to country.
