An offer can be legitimate and still involve risk, time, costs, and uncertain results. A pitch that hides those realities behind “easy money,” guaranteed income, or a deadline deserves careful scrutiny.

## What to examine

1. **The earnings claim.** Ask what the claim is based on, what costs are excluded, and whether typical outcomes are documented.
2. **The source of income.** If the main way to earn is recruiting participants or selling access rather than serving ordinary customers, step back and investigate.
3. **Upfront or recurring charges.** Training, inventory, coaching, subscriptions, or “activation” fees can change the real cost of the offer.
4. **Pressure to decide quickly.** A legitimate opportunity can be reviewed before you pay or sign.
5. **Vague work or product details.** You should be able to understand what is being sold, to whom, and how compensation is calculated.

## Illustrative scenario — not a case report

A social-media post says a paid coaching group will let you leave your job in a few weeks. It asks for an immediate fee and says the reward comes from building a “team,” while the actual product and costs are unclear. Do not treat testimonials or lifestyle images as evidence of typical results. Ask for written terms, total costs, and the basis for the earnings claim; walk away if those are unavailable.

## A cautious decision process

1. Take time before paying, signing, or inviting other people.
2. Search independently for the company or program name with “review,” “complaint,” and “scam.”
3. Calculate all costs, including recurring charges and the time you would need to spend.
4. Ask whether compensation comes from sales to ordinary customers rather than recruitment.
5. Discuss the offer with someone who does not benefit from your decision.

For investments, use the regulator and reporting rules that apply where you live. This guide is educational information, not financial advice.
