Romance scams use a relationship or the promise of one to make a money request feel personal. A profile, photo, or affectionate conversation is not proof that a person is who they say they are.

## Common patterns

1. **A quick move off the platform.** The person asks to continue in a private messenger, by text, or by email before you have had time to use the dating service's safety tools.
2. **An explanation for never meeting.** They say that work, travel, military service, an emergency, or distance makes a video call or in-person meeting impossible.
3. **A request built around a crisis.** After trust is built, they ask for help with a ticket, medical bill, visa, delivery, debt, or an investment opportunity.
4. **A payment method that is hard to reverse.** Gift-card codes, wire transfers, payment apps, cash, or cryptocurrency can make recovery difficult.
5. **Pressure to keep the relationship secret.** Isolation from friends or family makes it harder to get a second opinion.

## Illustrative scenario — not a case report

You match with someone who says they are temporarily working abroad. They message warmly every day, avoid a live video call, and later ask you to send money for an urgent travel problem. The useful response is not to diagnose the person from one detail: pause, do not send money, and verify through independent evidence before continuing.

## Pause, verify, act

1. **Do not send money or gifts** to an online love interest you have not met in person.
2. **Keep communication on the platform** until you are confident in the account and its safety features.
3. **Check independently.** Reverse-image-search a profile photo and search the stated job or story with the word “scam.” Mismatched names or repeated stories are warning signs, not proof by themselves.
4. **Ask someone you trust for a second view.** A friend can notice pressure or inconsistencies that are hard to see when you are emotionally involved.
5. **Report the account** to the dating service if it requests money or appears deceptive.

If you have already sent money or shared payment details, contact the payment provider or bank promptly and keep the messages and transaction record for a report to the relevant local authority.
